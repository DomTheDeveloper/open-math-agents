# Both formulas for OEIS A251328

Dominic A. Dabish (DomTheDeveloper), with Codex, November 27, 2026 (UTC)

R. H. Hardin’s sequence counts ordered arrays of height `n+1` and width two with entries in `{0,1,2}`. Every pair of consecutive rows must have total sum 2, 4, or 6. On November 29, 2018, Colin Barker proposed a generating function and a closed form for these counts. We derive both directly from the array definition.

## Count by the first row

Fix a particular first row and let $n$ be the number of rows still to be chosen. The number of completions depends on the first row only through its sum. Rows of sums 1 and 3 have identical successor counts, as do rows of sums 0 and 4. Write $o_n,e_n,m_n$ for the numbers of completions per fixed odd-sum, extreme-sum (0 or 4), and middle-sum (2) row respectively.

| First-row class | Number of possible first rows | Odd successors | Extreme successors | Middle successors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Odd | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| Extreme | 2 | 0 | 1 | 3 |
| Middle | 3 | 0 | 2 | 3 |

For example, a row of sum 0 can be followed by any of the three rows of sum 2 or by the single row of sum 4. A row of sum 4 has the analogous choices of sum 2 or 0. Reading the table with $v_n=(o_n,e_n,m_n)^T$ gives

$$
v_0=(1,1,1)^T,\qquad
v_{n+1}=Mv_n,\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}4&0&0\\0&1&3\\0&2&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
a_n=(4,2,3)v_n .
$$

Every valid nonempty array has a unique first row and suffix, so this multiplication counts every allowed array once. We extend the OEIS sequence down to $a_0=9$, the number of single rows. The next three totals are $a_1=39$, $a_2=171$, and $a_3=753$.

## The two identities

The characteristic polynomial of $M$ is $(t-4)(t^2-4t-3)=t^3-8t^2+13t+12$. Cayley–Hamilton gives

$$
a_{n+3}=8a_{n+2}-13a_{n+1}-12a_n\qquad(n\ge0).
$$

Let $A(x)=\sum_{n\ge1} a_n x^n$, starting at the OEIS offset. Multiplying by $1-8x+13x^2+12x^3$ kills every coefficient of degree at least four. The first four coefficients are $0,39,-141,-108$. The denominator has constant term one, so division is valid in formal power series and gives Barker’s first identity,

$$
A(x)=\frac{3x(13-47x-36x^2)}{(1-4x)(1-4x-3x^2)} .
$$

For the second identity let $r=\sqrt7$. The three roots of the characteristic polynomial are $4,2-r,2+r$. Hence the sequence

$$
b_n=\frac{56\,4^n+(35-13r)(2-r)^n+(35+13r)(2+r)^n}{14}
$$

satisfies the same recurrence. Substituting $n=0,1,2$ and $r^2=7$ gives $b_0=9$, $b_1=39$, $b_2=171$. The recurrence determines every subsequent term, so $a_n=b_n$ for all $n\ge0$. Since $56\,4^n=7\,2^{3+2n}$, this is Barker’s stated closed form.

## Independent numerical checks

A direct enumeration of all arrays gave 39, 171, 753, 3333, and 14823 for $n=1$ through $5$. The transfer calculation matched all 210 values in Hardin’s published table. The radical expression was also evaluated exactly in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt7]$ through $n=210$.
